# Hydraulic Resonance

Hydraulic Resonance begins with water as a field of relationships rather than a decorative surface. Several low-amplitude harmonic waves travel through a dark mineral plane, each with its own wavelength, direction, phase, and decay. Their interference makes the field feel alive without imitating stock ocean footage: broad frequencies establish a slow architectural rhythm, while finer ripples reveal depth and flow. The system is deterministic, seeded, and deliberately restrained, so every visit belongs to the same identity even though no individual frame appears completely still.

Scrolling sequences five families of architectural **outlines** within that moving field, in the same order as Effekt’s practice; cursor or touch movement supplies the frequency energy that makes the selected family appear. Baths and hammams come first through octagonal stones, radial niches, and the suggestion of a domed plan. Spas follow as concentric treatment zones, heated benches, and immersion basins; saunas use parallel timber slats and a compact heater block. Pools open into perimeter, gutter, and fountain contours, before hydraulic and wet-room lighting systems conclude the sequence as connected channels, nodes, outlets, and emitters. These are abstract plan-like outlines—not literal floor plans and not layouts of page content—so they evoke real work without inventing technical documentation.

The active outline is selected by a continuous scroll value rather than by swapping illustrations. As the reader moves, one distance field becomes available to the next chapter while labels and project context transition in real HTML above the canvas. The geometry does not simply sit on the surface, however: it remains latent until human input excites it. This separation lets the visual remain expressive while the information stays selectable, indexable, and understandable without the animation.

Pointer movement is the energy that draws the selected project outline out of the water. Position changes the phase centre and nearby wavelength, while velocity increases the contour’s coherence and reveal strength; moving across the field makes the hammam, spa, sauna, pool or fountain, and systems geometry resolve from interacting frequencies. Stillness reverses the process, allowing the outline to recede into ordinary moving water rather than remain as a permanent diagram. The response should feel as if controlled pressure has crossed a pool surface: immediate near the point of contact, slower across the full contour, and never so strong that the outline becomes a literal technical drawing. Touch input uses the same principle with a broader, gentler impulse, while keyboard navigation never depends on manipulating the field.

Craft is expressed through limits. Render only while the experience is visible, pause when the document is hidden, cap device-pixel ratio, and keep the shader’s harmonic and distance-field count fixed so the piece remains smooth on ordinary mobile hardware. When WebGL is unavailable, a static vector contour over a tonal water field preserves the idea. `prefers-reduced-motion` removes continuous drift and pointer impulses, presenting one composed outline with the full service sequence in adjacent text; the canvas is hidden from assistive technology and paired with an equivalent written description. The result is an entrance with atmosphere, but its motion always yields to legibility, performance, and user control.
